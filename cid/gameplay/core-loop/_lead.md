**Category:** Gameplay (stage 1) · **Wave:** 1 · Reads: `HANDOFF.md`, `CONCEPT.md`, `00-CORE.md`,

**What this domain is.** The loop's structure is closed by the brief and is not restated or
re-derived here. This index assigns **cadence**: when payoffs land, how big they are relative to
each other, how long a lap runs, how the curves are shaped, and where the dead-spots are.

**Two thirds of my `owns` line is closed before I start, and that is correct, not an omission:**

- **Tension is fixed at zero** and the brief instructs that nobody downstream invent any. I am
  designing **no** tension: no timer, no hazard, no soft-fail, no urgency, no scarcity, no
  streak-at-risk. What survives of "tension vs downtime" is the **downtime** half, assigned as
  sheet 08.
- **Streaks, dailies and pull-back hooks have no available instrument.** Daily rewards are
  priority 3; offline accrual is cut by permanence. **No sheet below proposes a hook.** The
  brief's answer is already on record ("an unfinished area and a half-empty index"), and what is
  left for me is the *cadence* side of it: what state a session must be able to stop in. That is
  sheet 09, and it reaches for nothing excluded.

## Why 9 sheets

The subject splits along two independent axes and I refused to fold them together. One axis is
**the payoff timeline**: how many events, how far apart, of which kinds, at what relative size
(01, 02, 03). The other is **the curves that produce them**: how long a lap runs, how areas grow
with depth, how upgrade costs grow, and the invariant relating those two growths (04, 05, 06,
07). Those are separate because Balance & Tuning can satisfy one and violate the other, and a
verifier needs to see which one broke. The remaining two are the parts of my `owns` line that
survive their own closures: downtime and its pathological form (08), and the session boundary,
which is all that is left of pull-back hooks once dailies and offline accrual are gone (09).

Sheet 07 exists as its own file rather than as a heading inside 05 or 06 because it is a
**relation** between two curves, not a curve, and because it is the one place in this domain
where I hold licence to overrule the brief (`[brief: soft]` assumption #2). A ruling that can
overturn an inherited assumption should not be buried in a sheet about something else. Sheet 03
is separate from 01 for the opposite reason: it resolves an ambiguity in the brief and carries
the game's highest-risk cadence requirement, so it must be cheap to redo if it fails
verification.

I did **not** create a sheet for the loop-in-one-line, for tension, or for streaks and dailies.
The first is closed by the brief and restating it would be re-deriving it; the other two are
closed by decisions above me and are stated as closed at the top of this index instead.

## Verification note

**Most likely to be contradicted: 04 `lap-length`, and by two different neighbours at once.**

First, by *persistence and storage work* [currently: Tech & Data]. If 04 rules that laps may span
sessions, then a partially cleared area must persist at patch granularity, which is precisely
the state `OPEN.md §2` warns "grows without bound unless areas are collapsed to a completion flag
once finished". A session-spanning lap keeps that state alive rather than collapsing it. I state
this as a consequence and do not resolve it: the *kind of work* is what-must-persist definition
[currently: Systems, stage 2] and how it is stored [currently: Tech & Data].

Second, by Balance & Tuning (stage 4), which owns the value and may find no value inside my
stated bound satisfies both 03's find requirement and 07's invariant. That is the correct place
for that collision to surface, which is why 04 must be written as a bound plus an acceptance
test rather than a preference.

**Runner-up: 03 `reveal-cadence`.** If *system definition work* [currently: Systems, stage 2]
reads loop row 2 literally, it will build reveal as a completion-gated batch grant and my
continuous-reveal cadence dies silently. G1 must therefore be resolved in 03 as an explicit
ruling with the two readings quoted, not as an assumption, and the consequence for
clear-on-contact feedback semantics [currently: Mechanics, stage 2] stated by name.

**Consequences this domain forces on neighbours, stated as consequences only.** Feedback
semantics at the moment of reveal, if reveals are continuous [Mechanics]. What partial-area state
must persist [Systems, then Tech & Data]. Whether reveal and completion remain the two audio
peaks, which my 02 ordering either confirms or overrides [Audio]. A maximum-gap requirement plus
"how close am I to done" legibility while moving, which points straight at the persistent HUD the
build stage cannot currently produce [UI/UX, and already flagged in `OPEN.md §4`]. Depth-1 area
sizing, which bounds the discovery-rate arithmetic [Meta & Content].

shares.

**What the research actually establishes, and it is the spine of sheets 04, 05 and 07.** In the
closest shipping analogue, a completion-shaped lap runs from a few minutes at the small end to
2–3.5 hours at the large end, with an early representative job around half an hour. That is
between roughly 1.5x and 10x this game's entire bound session, and the analogue survives it only
because (a) its audience plays long desktop sessions and (b) partial progress persists so a lap
spans sittings. This design shares (b) for free via permanence but has the opposite of (a):
10–20 minutes, mobile, ages 8–14. Two consequences that the sheets must decide rather than
assume: a depth-1 lap that closes inside one session is a **divergence** from the analogue and
has to be stated as a requirement; and at depth, laps will exceed a session, so either growth is
capped (05) or laps are session-spanning by design (04). The analogue also confirms the *shape*
of inherited assumption #2 (early small, late large, difficulty as duration) while giving no
evidence that it survives a 20-minute ceiling, which is exactly the question 07 has to answer.
