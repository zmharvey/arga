## Why 6 sheets

The domain looks thin because its headline is already written, and it is not. Ratifying the
existing sentence and inventing what the brief never wrote are two different acts, so they are
two sheets (`01`, `02`). The emotional promise is not one decision: the first-minute promise has
to survive with zero words and one payoff already seen, while the hour-ten promise has to survive
a familiar payoff, an unfinishable place, and no banked offline value, and the brief itself admits
that second one is weak. Same subject, opposite failure modes, so `03` and `04` are separate and a
single "emotional promise" sheet would have smeared the weak half into the strong half. `05` exists
because zero tension plus no mastery means most play-time is neither peak nor threat, and nothing
in the brief names what that time feels like; it is the only sheet that speaks to the ~90% of
minutes that are not a reveal. `06` is the fence: with six binding negatives spread across five
sheets of the brief, the guarantee list is worth stating once, positively, as promises to the
player rather than as prohibitions on designers, because that is the form other categories can
check their own work against. I considered folding `01` into `02` and `06` into `05` and rejected
both: ratification is not invention, and a source of pleasure is not a boundary on it.

| # | sheet | must decide |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | `fantasy-statement` | Which single sentence is the fantasy of record, ratified or overruled against the occupancy evidence below, and which register it commits to (custodial, archaeological, possessive, or another) given the brief runs three at once. |
| 02 | `what-accrues` | What the player becomes across a long play when power and skill are both ruled out: the from-and-to of their standing relative to the place, stated without naming the person or any rank. |
| 03 | `promise-first-minute` | The proposition a new player can hold after roughly one minute, arrived at with zero words, and which single expectation the first reveal is allowed to set. |
| 04 | `promise-hour-ten` | What the fantasy is still offering once reveals are familiar, the place is known to be endless, and nothing accrued while away: including what is promised about ever being finished. |
| 05 | `ambient-satisfaction` | The named feeling of being in the space between payoffs, specific enough that sound and visual work can aim at it and Tone can render it. |
| 06 | `never-happens` | The guarantee list: what this fantasy promises will never happen to the player or to work they have already done. |

### Rules that apply to all six

1. **No mechanic, number, rate, cost, curve or asset.** State a requirement on something you do
   not set, and name the kind of work that sets it.
2. **Wordless deliverability.** Any promise a sheet makes must be arrivable at from what happens
   on screen. If a sheet needs words, it states that as a delivery requirement and does not assume
   a surface exists (gap 7).
3. **Coin at most one term, and surface it.** Every term must reach the category's canonical term
   list *[currently Vocabulary Lead, this wave]*, spelled one way, or the category fails
   verification. Prefer reusing the brief's working words to inventing.
4. **Do not name a person, a rank, a place, a set, or a relic.** Those belong to role-and-cast
   work, world-scale work, set-theme work (wave 3) and object work (wave 4).
5. **No differentiation argument.** Making the fantasy the differentiator contradicts
   `[you chose: R1 Q1]`. The occupancy research below is defensive (do not promise what an
   occupant delivers better), never a licence to re-differentiate.
6. **2 to 4 checkable criteria per sheet.** For this domain that usually reads as a count, a
   presence or absence, or an observable state: *"this sheet names zero characters"*, *"the
   statement of record is one sentence and appears in N places"*, *"no promise in this sheet
   requires a word to be understood"*.

### Consequences for neighbouring subjects, for their owners to act on

- **Role-and-cast work** *[currently Identity Lead]*: `02` fixes a trajectory, so whatever noun is
  chosen for the player must be able to undergo it, and must not be a rank or title (no mastery).
- **World-scale work** *[currently Setting Lead]*: the reclamation fantasy requires that a cleared
  area be visibly, permanently different afterwards at area scale, not object scale. Gaps 5 and 6
  land on the singular-versus-endless contradiction that sheet owns.
- **Mood and register work** *[currently Tone Lead]*: `05` names a feeling, not a register. If
  *"warm, aged, unhurried"* is overruled, `05` breaks and should be revised, not silently kept.
- **Content-volume and discovery-rate work** *(wave 3)*: `04`'s promise is falsifiable by tuning.
  If a typical session can fail to produce a find, the promise is a lie. `03-META.md` already calls
  this *"the highest-risk tuning in the game"*.
- **Sound-design and visual-effect work** *(wave 4)*: `05` gives them a target feeling and nothing
  about execution.
- **Interface-surface work** *(wave 4)* and the build stage's pattern registry: gap 7 decides
  whether any of this reaches a player.

## Verification note

**`04-promise-hour-ten` is the sheet most likely to be contradicted later.** It depends on three
things it does not own: discovery rates per depth tier and set-completion bonuses (content-volume
work, wave 3), payoff cadence (core-loop work, wave 2), and whether the place is one ruin or many
(world-scale work, this wave). Any of the three can make its promise false without touching the
sheet. It is also the sheet where excluded systems are the tempting answer: *"why come back"* has
an obvious wrong answer in daily rewards, offline accrual and seasons, all priority 3, and
`[you chose: R2 Q2]` refused the *"seasons"* reframe by name. It must answer without them.

**Second most exposed: `02-what-accrues`,** to role-naming work this wave and to persistent-power-track
work in wave 2. If a power track is specced that reads as escalating strength, `02` and
*"not a power fantasy"* collide, and `02` is the softer of the two.

### The headline finding, and it qualifies a claim the brief makes twice

`00-CORE.md` and `research/landscape.md` state that *"no game in this family surfaced a
hidden-object collection layer"*. **True as scoped, and it does not survive one step outside the
family.** Harvest-or-dig into a permanent logbook, with a reward for completing a page or zone, is
mainstream on Roblox at very large scale:

- **DIG** (DIG Development, released 28 June 2025). Store copy: *"Uncover and collect hidden
  treasures, explore a massive open world, build unique loadouts with powerful charms."*
  **56,030,218 visits, 89.3% likes (101,475 up / 12,115 down), all-time peak 119,871 CCU, 88
  concurrent at time of fetch.** Its Collection is *"a detailed in-game logbook that records all
  the different types of digging up treasures and items caught"*, 601 items, and **completing a
  zone of the collection unlocks Mounts** (a completion bonus, structurally the brief's
  set-completion bonus). `[research: https://www.rolimons.com/game/126244816328678]`
  `[research: https://www.roblox.com/games/126244816328678/DIG]`
  `[research: https://dig-it-roblox.fandom.com/wiki/Collection]`
- **Fisch. ~4.5 billion visits, ~90% rating, 1.2M+ peak CCU.** Its Bestiary is *"a detailed,
  in-game logbook that records the different types of fish and items fishers have caught"*, with
  **per-page completion rewards** and threshold rewards at 70% and 100%.
  `[research: https://fischipedia.org/wiki/Bestiary]`
  `[research: https://fisch.fandom.com/wiki/Bestiary]`

**What this changes and what it does not.** It does not change where distinction lives: that is
`[you chose: R1 Q1]` and binding, and differentiation is a stated non-goal. It does mean the
collection layer is a **format players already know**, not a novelty, which is consistent with the
brief's own reference research (*"players of this will expect ... a visible collection of what they
have found"*). Consequence for `03` and `04`: the index cannot be presented as a surprise, and any
promise resting on the index being novel is `[cid: decided]` and wrong. **Flagged upward:** the
novelty framing in `00-CORE.md`'s *"Nothing in the surveyed family has this"* is narrower than it
reads.

### What the map says to the six sheets

- **The subject matter survives.** *"An overgrown ruin being reclaimed"* is, as far as I can
  verify, unoccupied as a shipping fantasy. `01` may ratify it without inventing a new noun, and a
  new noun would buy nothing anyway `[you chose: R1 Q1]`.
- **Three promises are occupied and must not be leaned on:** *satisfying before-and-after
  cleaning* (Carpet Cleaning Simulator is doing it now at 26.6M visits in a quarter), *filling an
  index* (Fisch, DIG), and *relaxed tending of a place that changes while you are away* (Grow a
  Garden, and unavailable here regardless).
- **The one property no occupant I found has is permanence at area scale.** Cleaning games
  re-dirty per job, dig sites refill, Fisch's water is inexhaustible, Grow a Garden's plot is
  growth rather than reclamation. That is `[you chose: R2 Q1]`, already binding, and it is the
  honest ground for `04`, `05` and `06`. Reading it as *the* differentiator would contradict
  `[you chose: R1 Q1]`, so it is offered as ground to stand on, not as a claim to make.
- **The reference's decay is the shape to expect** (10,435 to 819 peak-to-current while updated
  weekly). `04` writes against that, not against a hope.
