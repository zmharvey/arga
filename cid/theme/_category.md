## Scope gate

`03-META.md` priority 3, quoted whole:

> **Priority 3 — explicitly not in this project:**
> real procedural generation · rebirth · offline accrual · codes · daily rewards ·
> leaderboards · trading · seasons and events.
>
> `[I assumed — the ordering; scope was resolved through R4 Q1 and R5 Q1 but no explicit priority list was interviewed]`

**Relay the tag accurately.** The *list's ordering* is `[I assumed]`, and `OPEN.md §5` assumption 7
says everyone inherits it. But the individual exclusions are not all equally soft:

| excluded | how strongly |
|---|---|
| rebirth | `[brief: binding]` `[you chose: R2 Q2]`, and the *"seasons"* reframe was refused by name |
| offline accrual | binding by consequence: *"Nothing regrows, so nothing can accrue while away. Follows directly from permanence."* |
| real procedural generation | `[brief: binding]` `[you chose: R5 Q1]` — *"**Endless via shuffled authored chunks**, not generation."* |
| trading | covered by *"no mechanical interaction"* `[brief: soft]` `[you accepted: R6 Q2]` |
| codes · daily rewards · leaderboards · seasons and events | excluded **only** by the `[I assumed]` ordering, plus the `OPEN.md §2` live-ops default *"Ships and settles. No seasons or events."* |

**The instruction, and it applies to all six domains without exception: no domain may name, imply,
or build fiction around any priority-3 item.** Fiction is how excluded systems get smuggled back
in, because a story that implies a system creates pressure to build it. Concretely, this gate
forbids:

- any cyclical, seasonal, renewal, reincarnation or begin-again framing (imports rebirth, and the reframe was explicitly declined)
- any fiction in which the world changes, grows, or accumulates while the player is absent (imports offline accrual)
- any claim that the world creates or generates itself (imports procgen; the world is *authored chunks, shuffled*)
- any fiction of exchange, gifting, rivalry, ranking, or comparison between players (imports trading and leaderboards)
- any festival, holiday, anniversary, or calendar fiction (imports seasons and events)
- any fiction that presumes a daily visit, a streak, or a returning benefactor (imports daily rewards)

Two further "do not invent" instructions sit outside priority 3 and bind this category equally:

> *"**No mastery layer.** `[I assumed]` There is no execution skill in proximity-clearing to master. **Stated so nobody invents one.**"* (`03-META.md`)

> *"nobody downstream should invent tension to fill the gap."* (`02-GAMEPLAY.md`)

No fiction of skill, prowess, mastery, rank, or trial. The player does not get good at this.

## What verification will check (relayed from the workflow config, not my decision)

`cid/theme/_verified.md` runs on **opus** and checks:

> every term this category introduces appears once in the Vocabulary canonical list, spelled one way · no tone rule contradicts the fantasy, and no lore entry asserts a world rule Setting forbids · every named character in Identity has a faction or an explicit "unaffiliated" · nothing here specifies a mechanic, an economy or pacing number, or an art asset — relative orderings and content ratios stay with the domain that owns the subject · the tone sheets are specific enough to act on: a register and a humor level, not an adjective

Two consequences for how you work:

1. **Vocabulary is the last writer.** Its canonical list must contain every proper noun the other
   five introduce. Whichever order the six of you run in, the other five must surface every new term
   they coin in a form Vocabulary can collect. A term that exists in a Lore sheet and not in the
   canonical list fails the category.
2. **Every subagent sheet ends in 2 to 4 checkable criteria** (a value, a count, a state, or an
   observable behaviour). *"Feels satisfying"* fails. For a fiction category this usually means
   criteria of the form "term X appears in N places and nowhere else", "this sheet names zero
   characters", "no line of this copy exceeds N words". Each lead decides N subagents at runtime and
   numbers them `01`, `02`, `03`.

## Domains judged thin for this game, and why that is stated rather than silent

**Identity — thin, and deliberately so. Two of its four ownership slots are empty by
construction.** The brief's entire roster is 24 relics. There are no NPCs, enemies, vendors,
quest-givers, named characters, factions, or creatures in any sheet, and the social model is
*"no mechanical interaction"* `[you accepted: R6 Q2]`. Identity Lead is being told this rather than
left to discover it after planning a cast. It keeps real work (the player's role, which the brief
explicitly leaves open and which has zero interview coverage; and what a co-present stranger is in
the fiction), and it is instructed to report factions, NPC archetypes and relationships as empty.
**Verification: an Identity index that names zero characters is a correct result here, not a gap.**

**Lore — full latitude on history, one empty slot.** *"Why the conflict exists"* has no answer
because there is no conflict: no failure state, no antagonist, and an explicit handoff instruction
not to invent tension. Lore is also **delivery-starved** rather than idea-starved: no text surface
exists in the brief for it to reach the player through. Both stated so the lead does not manufacture
an antagonist or assume a lore screen.

**Fantasy — thin because the brief already wrote its headline.** The one-line fantasy, the emotional
promise, the audience motivations and the hook line are all on paper. Expect a short index. It is
**not** unnecessary: its mandated Roblox occupancy search at the fantasy level is work nobody else
in the pipeline will do, the brief's own research explicitly did not do it, and the brief's stated
nearest competitor (Treasure Hunt Simulator) has never been checked against this fantasy. That
search is the domain's justification.

**Setting, Tone and Vocabulary are full-weight.** Setting gets the largest genuinely blank page
(*"what the ruin actually is"*), Vocabulary the largest workload (*"all naming"*, plus an untagged
working vocabulary to adjudicate), and Tone the sharpest verification bar (*"a register and a humor
level, not an adjective"*) with its humor level already settled.

**No domain in this category is judged unnecessary.** Six were assigned and six will produce.

## Gaps in the brief this category hit

Passed upward, not filled.

**1. There is no surface for any of this category's output to reach the player.**
Onboarding is *"No text, no tutorial"* `[you accepted: R6 Q3]`. The screen list is
`collection-index`, `upgrades`, `areas`, `shop` with no lore or flavour surface.
`04-PRESENTATION.md` also notes *"**A persistent HUD does not fit**"* the build stage's one pattern.
Meanwhile the developer's in-session tone decision presupposes a **relic flavour text** field, and
constrains *"tutorial text"* the brief says does not exist. **Who decides:** Lore Lead and Tone Lead
each state a delivery requirement; **UI/UX (wave 4) decides whether the surface exists**, and
`ui-forge`'s pattern registry decides whether it can be built. If it cannot, this category's output
is unreachable and that is a finding about stage 0.

**2. The fiction of the collection is split across three categories in three different waves.**
`OPEN.md §4` gives *"all naming"* and *"what the four sets of relics mean"* to Theme & Narrative
(wave 1), *"Set themes"* to Meta & Content (wave 3), and *"The 24 relics"* to Art & Visuals
(wave 4). Wave 1 either pre-empts them or writes around a hole. **Instructed here:** Lore writes the
shape of meaning without naming the four themes; Vocabulary ships a pattern rather than 24 names.
**Who decides the arbitration:** not this category. Passed to whoever sequences CID, and to
Theme & Narrative Verification, which will see terms in wave 1 that wave 3 and wave 4 must match.

**3. "Who the player is" has zero interview coverage.**
`OPEN.md §1` lists no audit row for player role at all; it appears only as a left-open item. There
is no `[you said]`, `[you chose]` or `[you accepted]` anywhere in the brief on the subject.
Combined with a no-text onboarding, the least-anchored decision in this category is also the one
that can never be stated to the player. **Who decides:** Identity Lead, and it should record that it
is deciding without any developer input, so a later revision knows the decision was unanchored
rather than derived.

**4. No reading level or text budget exists for the 8-14 band.**
The band is binding `[you chose: R1 Q4]`, and `04-PRESENTATION.md` records that
*"extending to a text-free comprehension rule"* was **declined** as the broader version of the
accessibility pass. So text is permitted, but no sheet sets a reading level, a word budget, or a
per-label length. **Who decides:** Tone Lead and Vocabulary Lead each state their own requirement;
the global on-screen copy budget belongs to **UI/UX — Screens** (which owns *"on-screen copy"*) and
is not settled anywhere.

**5. Whether relic rarity exists as a concept separate from overgrowth rarity is unresolved.**
`01-FOUNDATION.md` says *"**Rarity ladder lives in the overgrowth**, not in a separate drop table"*
`[I assumed — carried from the reference's model; not interviewed]`, and `OPEN.md §5` lists it as
assumption 1 inherited by *"Art, Systems"*. But `03-META.md` says deeper areas *"hide rarer sets"*
and `01-FOUNDATION.md` says *"rarer finds"*, which reads as relics having rarity too. Vocabulary
cannot know whether it needs one rarity ladder or two. **Who decides:** Systems (wave 2) and
Meta & Content (wave 3), per `OPEN.md §5`. Vocabulary Lead must state a conditional requirement
rather than guess.

**6. The place is singular in the prose and endless in the design.**
*"reclaim a ruin"*, *"what the ruin actually is"* (singular) against *"Endless via shuffled authored
chunks"* and priority 2's *"visitable restored **ruins**"* (plural). The brief never reconciles
scale. **Who decides:** Setting Lead. Named here because it is a genuine inconsistency in the source
rather than latitude the brief intended to leave, and Setting should know it is resolving a
contradiction rather than filling a blank.
