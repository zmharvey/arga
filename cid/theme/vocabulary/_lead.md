Twelve gaps. Each is routed to the sheet that must decide it, or out of my subject by kind of

## Why 11 sheets

The workload here is not one naming pass, it is three different jobs that happen to use words:
**adjudicating inherited vocabulary** (the brief's working words carry no provenance, and one
thing has two names), **inventing term slots for the seven countable things this game actually
has** (one currency, one collectible class, one container, four sets, N areas, one action, three
axes, one rarity ladder), and **building the machinery that keeps all of it single-spelled across
54 other domains in five waves**. Those are separated below because they fail differently: the
adjudication fails by deferring to habit, the invention fails by borrowing an occupied word, and
the machinery fails by having no intake format. Within the invention group I split per *thing
named* rather than per *screen* or per *system*, because a sheet that names one thing can be
re-run cheaply when the thing changes, and because the two highest-risk terms — the collectible
class and the rarity ladder — each collide with a different downstream wave and should not be
able to take a third term down with them. Rules (02) and bans (03) are separate because the first
describes what a good name looks like and the second is the checkable list that cross-category
verification will run; a ban list buried inside a rules essay does not get run. The register (11)
is last and separate because it is a protocol, not a naming decision, and because five other
leads in this category plus every later wave must submit to it.

## Verification note

**Sheet 05 (`collectible-class`) is the most likely to be contradicted, and the contradicting
party is downstream habit rather than a downstream decision.** `relic` is the brief's own word in
three sheets, it is in a screen slug, and it is the genre's ambient word — so every later wave
will reach for it by reflex. If sheet 01 and 05 rule against it, the contradiction arrives as
*drift*, in the 24-object sheets (Art & Visuals, wave 4) and the store description (Discovery &
Marketing, wave 5), not as an argument. That is the failure mode cross-category verification
exists to catch, and it is why sheet 11's intake format matters more than it looks.

**Second most likely: sheet 10 (`rarity-ladder`), contradicted by rarity-table work in waves 2–3**,
which may return one ladder where the sheet assumed two or four steps where it assumed three. It
ships conditional for exactly this reason.

**Third: sheet 01 itself, contradicted by me being wrong about latitude.** If a reviewer holds
that the brief's repeated use of a working word constitutes a decision, every ruling in 01 becomes
an overrule of a `[brief: soft]` item rather than a `[cid: decided]` filling of a gap. I have
recorded them as `[cid: decided]` because no naming provenance tag exists anywhere in the source,
and I have named the ambiguity here rather than resolving it in my own favour silently.
