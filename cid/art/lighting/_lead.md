## Why 2 sheets

**One contract key, so one value sheet; plus one constraint sheet, because the floor is a
procedure and a procedure is not a value.** `01` is `lighting`: every property of the single
state, the count of states, the zero-counts, and the route by which any of it reaches a place.
That is one decision — what this world is lit by — and splitting it by property would produce
four sheets that all reach for the same manifest and collide in the merger. `02` decides
something genuinely different: **how a critic fails a scene against the floor**, which is a
method and a threshold rather than a light value, and it carries a requirement outward onto
geometry I do not own. It constrains a key I own (it is the reason `01` may not raise
`Brightness` to fix a dim vault) and it therefore belongs here rather than in Environment. It
carries no `manifest` block, on the precedent of `tech/performance/03`, which supplies no key,
says so, and passes both gates.

**Considered and not assigned, each with the ruling that closed it:**

- **A second lighting state, a day cycle, a golden hour, a dusk key art.** Contradicted by the
  fiction, not merely unbudgeted: `theme/setting/03` `R1` and `R4` make *nothing in this place a
  function of time*, and a sky getting later is the plainest trend a place can have. Its four
  arguments stand: it would put `Sundial`, `Gnomon`, `Vane` and `Orrery` into service as
  instruments; a cycle either holds the luma floor at every hour (and is decorative) or breaches
  a stated requirement for part of every session; two inherited absences already forbid an
  advancing dial; and it is a second complete state for parts that are inside vaults.
- **Weather looks.** `R2`, zero, on four grounds of its own. No sheet.
- **Time-of-day and weather as a live-ops or seasonal channel.** Priority 3 by construction:
  *"seasons and events"* is excluded, and `theme/setting/03` already states the consequence —
  *"there is no hour, weather or season channel to run anything through."* **I assign nothing
  here and reserve no field, no property and no enum value for it.**
- **Colour grading as a post-effect.** The engine's grading instruments are
  `ColorCorrectionEffect`, `BloomEffect`, `SunRaysEffect`, `BlurEffect` and `DepthOfFieldEffect`,
  and the category's row 27 lists *"colour grade post-effect, vignette"* as **[nothing]** on
  `D2`. **The grade that survives is not a post-effect**: it is `Ambient`, `OutdoorAmbient`,
  `ColorShift_Top`/`_Bottom` and `ExposureCompensation`, which are properties of the one state
  and live in `01`. A separate grading sheet would be one decision described twice.
- **Fog and depth.** `N7` forbids it and gives the grep. The *values* that make "no fog" checkable
  (`FogStart`, `FogEnd`, `FogColor`) are three rows in `01`'s table, not a sheet.
- **Lighting setup per area.** There is one state, so there is no per-area setup to write.
  `theme/setting/03` pre-authorised a **per-part constant hour** as escalation step 3 and the
  category brief says I *"may name as available and may not take"* it — `01` names it as
  available, unspent, and states that taking it revises that sheet's criterion 1. **No sheet
  spends it.**
- **A lighting performance budget.** `does_not_own` — Tech & Data, Performance.

### My ruling on one state versus more, and the argument

**One state, and I am not pushing back on my category lead.** The interesting objection is the
one I was handed: four depths and eight areas under one state means depth 4 looks exactly like
depth 1. **It does not, and the reason is mechanical rather than a matter of taste.**

1. **Depth already reads through three channels that are not light.** `layout` ships **four
   disjoint chunk families** — `terrace`, `cistern`, `vault`, `spire` — that *"share no chunk"*,
   at 35/37/39/40 patch anchors per chunk. `depths` ships eight areas at 140 → 640 patches. The
   quantity of green rises with depth by ruling (`theme/setting/01`: *"only the quantity of green
   varies"*). Depth 1 is a walled open platform; depth 3 is the inside of a vault. **Those are
   different places before a single light value changes.**
2. **The engine renders that difference from one state for free.** `Ambient` applies indoors and
   out; `OutdoorAmbient` applies outdoors only and the engine decides by surface exposure
   `[research: create.roblox.com/docs/environment/lighting]`. The shipped `Ambient`
   `[0.32,0.31,0.27]` is darker than the shipped `OutdoorAmbient` `[0.45,0.43,0.38]`, so a
   vaulted bay is *already* lit differently from a terrace **under the same five values**. A
   second state would be buying, with a whole extra lighting configuration and a state count of
   2, something authored geometry supplies at zero cost.
3. **So the real risk runs the other way, and that is what `02` exists for.** The danger is not
   that depths look identical; it is that depths 2–3 fall **below** the floor, because darkening
   both stone and green compresses their contrast ratio toward 1:1 and the tier signal is worth
   *"a core economic signal"* `[brief: soft, effectively binding]`. A second, brighter vault
   state would be the reflex fix and it is exactly what `theme/setting/03` forbids: *"you may not
   solve a dim vault by moving the sun."*
4. **And the budget confirms it rather than carrying it.** Every ceiling is `[playtest unknown]`,
   so a budget argument alone would be a claim against a number nobody has measured. I do not
   rest the ruling on one. What I do state: a second state is a second complete configuration for
   every part and it buys a difference that is already there.

**What this costs, said plainly:** if playtesting finds the eight areas read as one place, the
escalation order is already written and pre-authorised — stone hue and ornament inside the band
first, then canopy motion beyond the built edge, then a **per-part constant hour** fixed for a
whole lap. Steps 1 and 2 are other domains' existing budgets. Step 3 revises
`theme/setting/03` criterion 1 and is not mine to take.
