# 01 — Foundation

**Layer 1 — foundation.** Everything else in the game is downstream of this sheet. Anyone
working on theme, fiction, tone or the loop creates *within* these constraints; the names,
the copy and the tuning are theirs to invent.

## Theme / fantasy

**Direction: clearing snow, and pushing back a storm.** [you accepted: R1 Q2]

The chosen fantasy over three alternatives that were declined — absorb-and-grow,
reclaim-the-ruin, reap-the-spirits. Recorded because a declined direction is context:
the tone here is *not* horror, and *not* a growth power fantasy.

What the direction fixes:

- **A before/after promise.** The satisfaction is a space visibly going from buried to
  clear. This is the emotional core and every department should serve it.
- **A natural rarity ladder** in the material itself — powder → packed → ice → crystal,
  or whatever Theme names it. Rarity is a property of the *snow*, not of a separate
  drop table. [you accepted: R1 Q2, ladder shape research-derived]
- **Zones read as escalating cold**, so progression is legible without a tutorial: a
  yard, then somewhere colder, then somewhere hostile.
- **Tone: low-stress and warm-hearted, not grim.** The genre's appeal is relaxation
  [research: audience motivations], and the reference is explicitly "a relaxing
  lawn-trimming simulator" [research:
  https://www.roblox.com/games/133086043677134/Grass-Incremental-Simulator]. A blizzard
  is a *backdrop with stakes*, not a horror threat.

Seasonal-discovery risk was raised against this direction and **consciously accepted** —
it only bites on revenue, which is not the goal. [you accepted: R1 Q3]

Left open — the setting's identity and name, who the player
is in the fiction beyond the category settled in R2, the snow-tier names, zone names,
and whether there is any narrative framing at all. *[currently: Theme & Narrative]*

## Core loop

Inherited from the reference nearly unchanged — that inheritance is the point.
[you said]

| # | player action | produces |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | move through snow; it clears on contact | currency, scaled by the snow's rarity tier |
| 2 | spend currency on the three upgrade axes | faster clearing — more value, more spawn, wider radius |
| 3 | clear enough to hit a rebirth threshold | the option to reset |
| 4 | rebirth | permanent multiplier currency; upgrades and currency reset |
| 5 | spend multipliers, unlock the next colder zone | denser/rarer snow, a new stacking multiplier |

**How it closes:** the zone unlocked in step 5 supplies denser and rarer snow to step 1,
so each lap makes the next lap's raw material better. Rebirth multipliers compound
across laps. It is a genuine loop, not a funnel. [research: skeleton] [I assumed — the
explicit closure statement; the reference implies it but no source states it]

**Escalation — what changes between lap 1 and lap 100:** early laps are *spawn-rate
limited* (not enough snow), late laps are *radius and value limited* (plenty of snow,
not enough reach). The binding constraint moving from patience to build is what keeps
the loop from going flat. [you accepted: step 6 Q3 — originally extrapolated from the
upgrade axes with no source; confirmed. A radius-limited-throughout curve was the
alternative and was declined as too flat.]

**Lap length:** target 45–90 seconds for an early rebirth cycle, lengthening as
multipliers stack. **Deliberately provisional.** [you accepted: step 6 Q2 — you chose to
keep it as a hypothesis rather than fix it, because research could not verify the
reference's pacing: the guide source returned HTTP 405 and I did not substitute a guess.]

**Left open — treat this number as something to test, not to hit.** It is the one
figure in this document with no evidence behind it, and 20–40s and 2–4min were both
offered and declined in favour of leaving it open.

Left open — the actual curves. Cost scaling per axis, rebirth threshold
formula, zone multiplier values, and where the pacing dead-spots are. *[currently: Core Loop]*
