## Purpose

**To exercise the `arga` pipeline end to end on an idea nobody has pre-solved.**
[simulated: R1 Q3]

Success is the stage-0 gates firing on a brief written in good faith, and CID wave 1 running
against the result. Not players, not revenue.

**Non-goals:** shipping this; beating either incumbent; revenue.

## Where distinction comes from

**Shared risk, and real loss.** [simulated: R1 Q1]

The idea as brought is occupied in full. Bidding on unopened lots, the partial peek through
the door gap, live player-versus-player bidding and a haul capacity limit are **all shipping
today** in a game one month old with a 47,940 peak CCU.
[research: https://gamelandinsider.com/storage-hunters-open-world-guide]
[research: https://www.rolimons.com/game/98800969324557]

Six games were checked across auctions, thrift resale, pawn shops and blind boxes. **Two gaps
survived:**

1. **Every occupant is solo.** One player bids, one player profits. No shared bid, no split
   proceeds, anywhere. [research: `research/landscape.md`]
2. **The leading occupant has no permanent downside.** Its progression is reinvestment cycles
   and rising limits, *"with no mention of permanent setbacks or resource loss mechanics."*
   Overpaying shrinks a margin; it never sets you back.
   [research: https://gamelandinsider.com/storage-hunters-open-world-guide]

So the design is: **you cannot afford the good lots alone, the split is agreed before the
reveal, the game enforces it, and the syndicate can lose.**

**Consequence downstream:** the negotiation surface and the reputation system are the primary
creative work. The item roster is secondary — it exists to make lots valuable, and both
incumbents already prove items alone are not a differentiator.

## Audience

**13+, socially motivated, mixed device, medium-long sessions.** [simulated: R1 Q4]

**The age band is forced by a mechanic, not chosen for taste.** Negotiating a split requires
real conversation, and Roblox's chat filtering for under-13 accounts makes free-text
negotiation unreliable. A game whose core skill is talking cannot target the band that cannot
talk freely. This is the one audience decision in the brief that is a consequence rather than
a preference.

- ~50% mobile / ~45% desktop / ~5% console [simulated: R1 Q4 — the split is assumed; the band
  is a consequence]. **More desktop-weighted than a typical Roblox game** because typing
  matters.
- 20–35 minute sessions, longer than the incumbents' 27.9 minute average, because a syndicate
  takes time to assemble.
- motivated by **negotiation, reading people, and shared jeopardy** — not by collection.

Declined: 9–12 (cannot chat freely, which removes the game); 16+ trading-economy enthusiasts
wanting a full market (too much system for the scope).

## Genre

**Negotiation, wearing auction clothes.** [simulated: R1 Q1]

Stated in this order deliberately. A player arriving from Storage Hunters will recognise every
verb and be surprised by the skill required. That is the intended effect and also the main
retention risk: it looks like a game they have played.

Closest vocabulary match in the closed genre list is `delivery-logistics` for its
contract shape and `roleplay` for its emergent-negotiation shape, and **neither fits.**
Recorded as a gap in the vocabulary rather than forced into a wrong bucket.
