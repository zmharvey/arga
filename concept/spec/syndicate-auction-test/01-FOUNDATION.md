## Theme / fantasy

**A market-floor operator who reads people as much as lots.** [simulated: R2 Q2]

You are not a treasure hunter and not a shopkeeper. You are someone who spends their day at
auctions working out who is bluffing, who is good for their share, and what a closed door is
worth.

What the direction fixes:

- **Other players are the subject matter**, not decoration. The interesting object in the room
  is the person bidding next to you.
- **The lot is a MacGuffin.** What is inside matters only because it settles who was right.
  Do not build the game around item fantasy; both incumbents already own that ground.
  [research: `research/landscape.md`]
- **Ordinary and slightly grubby.** Concrete, chain-link, strip lights. No mystique, no
  cursed-artifact framing.
- **No combat, no theft, no cheating each other.** The tension is entirely economic and social.

Left open — the setting's specifics, what the lots contain in fiction, who the auctioneer is,
all naming. *[kind of work: naming and fiction]*

## Tone

**Wry and transactional.** [simulated: R2 Q1]

Asked as its own item. A market-floor game can be played as grim (debt, desperation), comic
(cartoon hustlers) or wry (competent people enjoying the game), and those are three different
products.

| dimension | decision |
|---|---|
| register | dry, conversational, a little clipped. The game talks like a trader, not a narrator. |
| **humor level** | **present and dry, and it may appear in system copy** — unusually for this pipeline. Haggling is inherently funny and a straight-faced auction game would read as cold. **Never at a player's expense**, and never in an error or a loss message. |
| seriousness | the money matters; nobody is ruined. Losses sting and pass. |
| emotional beats | two peaks — **the reveal** (the lot opens and everyone learns together) and **a syndicate paying out well.** Nothing else peaks. |

**Tonal do-nots:** no desperation or poverty framing, no mockery of a losing player, no
cursed-treasure mystique, no corporate-satire register.

**The reveal is a shared peak, which is unusual.** In both incumbents the reveal is private. Here
three or four players learn at the same instant, so it should be built as a group moment.

## Core loop

**Interviewed as its own round.** [simulated: R1 Q2]

| # | player action | produces |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | peek at the lot through the door gap | partial, ambiguous information |
| 2 | form or join a syndicate and **agree a split** | a binding contract, made before anyone knows the contents |
| 3 | bid as a syndicate against other syndicates and solo bidders | the lot, or nothing |
| 4 | win, open the lot, reveal | the contents, seen by all members at once |
| 5 | the game enforces the agreed split | your share |
| 6 | sell your share | currency |
| 7 | your partner reputation updates | who will syndicate with you next |

**A lap is one lot,** peek to split. [simulated: R1 Q2]

**How it closes:** step 7 changes who will work with you in step 2. Good partners get access to
bigger syndicates, which afford better lots, which pay more. **Reputation is the progression
track**, not cash.

**Lap 1 versus lap 100:** early lots are cheap enough to take alone, so the game teaches the
solo loop first. Later lots are unaffordable alone, so the binding constraint moves from *cash*
to *who will have you.*

**Lap length: unknown.** No auction-timer or bid-increment figure could be sourced across three
source kinds (Fandom 402, techwiser 307, guide site qualitative only). The incumbent's average
session is 27.9 minutes, which bounds it loosely and no more.
[research: https://www.rolimons.com/game/98800969324557] *[kind of work: all numeric curves]*

## The divergence from the opening idea

The idea proposed bidding on unopened units and selling the contents. **Research found that
shipping, twice**, including the partial-peek and live-PvP-bid details that felt original.

**Two things changed:**

1. **The bid became collective.** Nothing in the family shares a bid or splits proceeds.
2. **Loss became real.** The leading occupant cannot set a player back; here it can.

**A cost was accepted.** Both changes make the game harder to understand from a thumbnail, and
it will be mistaken for the incumbents by players who then find a different game underneath.
That is a retention risk, not a marketing one, and `05-OUTWARD.md` carries it.
